01 — THE CONSTITUTIONAL INVARIANT

A learner does not master a concept because they opened it.

Most learning software confuses exposure with understanding. A lesson opened, a video watched, a page scrolled to the bottom — none of it is evidence that a learner can actually do the thing. Emeritus starts from the opposite premise: a concept only advances when a learner has demonstrated it, and that demonstration is graded by a trusted server, not a browser event.

This shows up immediately once you look at a single learner's numbers side by side. School grade, demonstrated mastery, retention, and readiness are four different measurements, and they are allowed to disagree — because in a real classroom, they usually do.

SCHOOL GRADE 92%
DEMONSTRATED MASTERY 76%
RETENTION (90-DAY) 71%
READINESS FOR NEXT UNIT 73%

Illustrative learner profile. Emeritus never collapses these into a single number — each is computed independently from evidence, and the gaps between them are exactly what educators and guardians need to see.

02 — THE RUNTIME

Every topic runs the same loop, at a pace the learner can bear.

No screen, subject, or grade skips this sequence. What changes by grade band is the modality — how a six-year-old proves understanding looks nothing like how a junior in AP Government does, but both pass through the same evidence gate.

  1. 01

    Diagnose

    Check prerequisites and prior evidence before teaching anything new — repeating a lesson the learner already has is wasted time.

  2. 02

    Teach

    Deliver instruction at the reading level, density, and modality the pedagogy policy engine determines for this learner, right now.

  3. 03

    Show & interact

    Illustrations, diagrams, manipulatives, and simulations the learner can manipulate — not decoration, but a teaching surface in its own right.

  4. 04

    Comprehend

    A checkpoint before independent practice: recognition, recall, or application, matched to what's developmentally reasonable to ask.

  5. 05

    Practice & apply

    Guided practice fades into independent application, with confidence captured alongside correctness wherever that's meaningful.

  6. 06

    Assess

    Server-side evaluation only. A learner's browser is never the source of truth for whether an answer was right.

  7. 07

    Master — or remediate

    Strong evidence advances the learner. Weak evidence routes to misconception detection and a different instructional strategy — not the same lesson again.

  8. 08

    Retain

    Mastered concepts re-enter the loop on a spaced schedule. Mastery that isn't retained is downgraded — automatically, not punitively.

03 — INTELLIGENCE LAYER

Six engines decide what happens next — none of them decide it alone.

Every adaptive decision in Emeritus is advisory until evidence confirms it. No engine can write a mastery state; only evaluated learner responses can.

ENGINE 01

Pedagogy Policy

Determines language complexity, visual density, interaction type, and scaffolding for this learner, this concept, this moment — never a global difficulty slider.

ENGINE 02

Comprehension & Confidence

Models understanding across recognition, recall, application, analysis, reasoning, and transfer — and pairs correctness with confidence to separate fragile knowledge from real gaps.

ENGINE 03

Mastery State Machine

Moves a concept through NEW → LEARNING → DEVELOPING → PROFICIENT → MASTERED → RETAINED, with every transition requiring evidence, not elapsed time.

ENGINE 04

Misconception & Prerequisite Diagnosis

Treats a wrong answer as a clue, not a failure — distinguishing a careless slip from a genuine misconception from a gap two grades back.

ENGINE 05

Adaptive Remediation

Changes instructional strategy on repeated struggle — text to visual, visual to interactive, interactive to worked example — before ever repeating the same lesson.

ENGINE 06

Retention & Spaced Review

Schedules review on a configurable curve and lets mastery legitimately decay into REVIEW_DUE or AT_RISK when evidence says it should.

04 — DEVELOPMENTAL PEDAGOGY

One curriculum graph. Four entirely different learning experiences.

The modality changes by grade band; the evidence standard never does.

K–2

See it, touch it, say it

Understanding is shown through pointing, matching, sorting, and speaking — not typing.

Leads with illustrations, read-aloud, manipulatives, drag-and-drop, short interactions, immediate feedback.

3–5

Read, reason, explain

Independent reading and multi-step reasoning enter, supported by visual models learners can lean on.

Leads with comprehension, vocabulary, visual models, maps, timelines, guided written response.

6–8

Argue with evidence

Abstract reasoning and structured writing take over — claims now need to be defended with data.

Leads with mathematical modeling, experiments, data interpretation, evidence-based argument, projects.

9–12

Work like the discipline works

Complex texts, laboratory reasoning, formal writing, and exam-grade rigor — building toward college and career readiness.

Leads with primary sources, research, source evaluation, simulation, formal argumentation, AP-level assessment.

05 — THREE EXPERIENCES, ONE TRUTH

Students, educators, and guardians see the same evidence — shaped for what each of them needs to do with it.

STUDENT

What should I learn today?

  • A daily plan built from weak mastery, due reviews, and upcoming assessments — not a static syllabus
  • A knowledge map showing what's mastered, developing, at risk, or locked by prerequisite
  • A curriculum-grounded AI tutor that coaches instead of completing the work
EDUCATOR

Where does the class actually stand?

  • Concept and misconception heatmaps built from real attempt data, not self-reported progress
  • Standards-mastery reporting derived from evidence, mapped to the frameworks you already use
  • Authoring and pacing tools with final authority to override any system recommendation
GUARDIAN

Is my child actually keeping up?

  • A concise view of strengths, priority gaps, and retention risk — not raw telemetry
  • Access strictly limited to explicitly authorized guardian–learner relationships
  • Visibility into upcoming assessments and what's genuinely due for review
06 — AI TUTOR GOVERNANCE

A curriculum-grounded coach, deliberately kept on a short leash.

The tutor always knows the learner's grade, current objective, standards, mastery, and active misconceptions. What it's permitted to do with that context is a hard boundary, not a prompt-level suggestion.

THE TUTOR DOES

  • Guide reasoning with questions before giving answers
  • Offer hints scaled to grade and current struggle
  • Re-explain a concept through a different modality
  • Generate fresh practice grounded in the curriculum graph
  • Reference the learner's actual evidence and remediation history

THE TUTOR NEVER

  • Completes graded work on a learner's behalf
  • Writes a final essay or impersonates a student
  • Reveals protected assessment answers
  • Writes a mastery state directly — only evaluated evidence can
  • Makes unsupported high-stakes educational decisions alone

FAQ

Questions we get before a pilot starts.

How is this different from an LMS or a video-based platform?+
An LMS tracks whether content was opened. Emeritus tracks whether a concept was demonstrated, through server-evaluated evidence, and keeps tracking whether that understanding survives over time. It's closer to an assessment and mastery engine that happens to deliver instruction than a content library with a gradebook bolted on.
Does the AI ever decide a student has mastered something on its own?+
No. AI proposes — hints, explanations, remediation strategy, curriculum drafts. Mastery state only changes in response to evaluated learner evidence, computed server-side. This is a hard architectural boundary, the same invariant we hold across every product Zenith AI builds in regulated or high-stakes domains: advisory AI, human- and evidence-gated outcomes.
What happens when a learner gets something wrong?+
The system first asks whether it's a careless slip, a misreading, or a real gap — and if it's a gap, whether it's in this concept or a prerequisite one or two grades back. Remediation changes instructional strategy (text → visual → interactive → worked example → guided practice) rather than simply replaying the same lesson.
How does mastery decay, and why would you want that?+
Mastered concepts are scheduled for spaced review — 1, 3, 7, 14, 30, 60, 90 days, adjusted by evidence. A concept that fails retrieval moves to REVIEW_DUE or AT_RISK. This is deliberate: a mastery badge that never expires stops meaning anything, and readiness for the next unit depends on what's actually retained, not what was once true.
Can a guardian see everything a student does?+
Only through an explicitly authorized guardian–learner relationship, and only a concise readiness view — strengths, priority gaps, retention risk, upcoming assessments — not raw event-level telemetry. Object-level authorization is enforced at the platform layer, not assumed by the interface.
What does a school need to have in place before a pilot?+
A course roster, the standards framework you report against, and one subject willing to run a real vertical slice end-to-end. We certify grade-band and subject pilots individually before expanding, following the same evidence discipline the platform itself enforces on learners.
